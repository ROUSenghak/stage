\begin{abstract}
This report is the \textbf{detailed technical reference} for the internship
work. It is intended to document the concrete implementation choices, data
audit, preprocessing rules, intermediate diagnostics, renewal-linking logic,
and survival-modeling outputs in a form that can be checked, reproduced, and
handed off. BOAMP notices do not contain an explicit field identifying whether
a call-for-tender (\textit{appel d'offre}) renews a previous contract. The
renewal relationship must therefore be inferred from observable signals: buyer
identity, contract description, CPV classification, and timing relative to the
expected contract end date.

\textbf{Phase 1.} Starting from 1,933 \textit{APPEL\_OFFRE} notices over
2015--2024, the pipeline defines 1,210 eligible source contracts and links 665
of them to a later renewal candidate, giving a final linking rate of 55.0\%.
The linking algorithm uses Sentence-Transformer embeddings with a four-component
composite score. The official Phase-2 modeling input,
\texttt{boamp\_phase2\_survival.csv}, contains one row per eligible source
contract with a binary \texttt{event} indicator and an
\texttt{observed\_duration\_months} survival time.

\textbf{Phase 2.} Survival analysis on the 1,210-contract dataset (665 events,
545 right-censored) yields a global Kaplan--Meier median survival of 50.1 months.
The Cox proportional hazards model achieves an in-sample concordance of 0.6317 (C-index);
the best-fitting parametric model is log-normal with AIC = 7\,068.4 and
concordance 0.6548. Statistically significant predictors are
\texttt{declared\_duration\_months} (HR = 0.991, $p < 0.001$) and
\texttt{start\_year} (HR = 1.071, $p < 0.001$). A sensitivity analysis
restricting to high-confidence links (composite $\geq 0.50$, 400 events)
yields a Cox concordance of 0.616 (KM median not reached, event rate 33.1\%), supporting
the stability of the main qualitative conclusions under a stricter event definition. Finally, the survival model is
translated into operational 12- and 24-month renewal-risk indicators at contract,
buyer, and segment level --- probabilities of an identifiable BOAMP renewal under
the proxy-linking rule, intended as prioritisation indicators rather than legally
verified renewal probabilities.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Calibration update --- 2026-07-05}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Calibration update --- 2026-07-05}

The 665-event output described in the original sections is now treated as the
\textbf{pre-calibration baseline}, not the recommended input. This section
documents why that baseline was replaced and how the replacement was built,
validated, and applied.

\paragraph{Initial method and challenge discovered.}
The original linking rule (Section~\ref{sec:composite}) accepted any
candidate with a non-negative composite score after a soft text-similarity
filter, which maximized the linking rate (665/1{,}210, 55.0\%) but could not be
checked against ground truth: BOAMP carries no certified renewal-chain label.
A manual audit of 150 stratified cases against the full official BOAMP record
(2026-07-02) found raw precision of only $\approx$0.15 (weighted
$\approx$0.09 over the 665-event population), rising to 0.50 in the
high-confidence tier and to 1.00 only above text similarity 0.80; 12.8\% of
decided-unlinked sources also hid a plausible missed renewal
(Section~\ref{sec:manual_audit}). The baseline optimized for linking rate,
not for link correctness, and no legal ground truth exists to certify any
individual real link. This is the reason the event definition was recalibrated:
a synthetic BOAMP benchmark --- scored with the same Sentence-Transformer
encoder as the real pipeline
(\nolinkurl{paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2}) --- and real BOAMP
diagnostics were used to choose three explicit event-definition rules:

\paragraph{Why a synthetic benchmark?}
Real BOAMP does not provide certified renewal-chain labels, so the linking task
cannot be evaluated on the real data with true precision and recall. The
synthetic benchmark was therefore created to provide a controlled evaluation
dataset where the true recurrence relation is known by construction, while still
reflecting the empirical difficulties seen in BOAMP. Its role is methodological:
to test and calibrate the proxy-event definition before using that definition in
survival analysis.

\paragraph{How it was built.}
The synthetic benchmark is fully implemented as a simulation-based BOAMP-like
dataset where recurrence truth is known by construction. It generates synthetic
source notices, synthetic candidate notices, and an explicit true-link table in
three scenarios (easy, medium, hard), with design parameters guided by the real
BOAMP data. The simulation intentionally reproduces the main observed failure
modes of the linking task: buyer-name variation, generic or drifting CPV codes,
missing CPV or duration, short or paraphrased text, timing shifts around the
expected end date, and ambiguous large-buyer same-name candidates. The same
Sentence-Transformer encoder and the same scoring logic used in the operational
pipeline are then applied to the synthetic pairs.

\paragraph{What the benchmark shows.}
Because the truth is synthetic, the benchmark is not presented as real BOAMP
validation; instead it is a controlled stress test that allows direct
computation of precision, recall, and F1. Under the final Sentence-Transformer
scoring, the balanced rule performs strongly in the easy scenario (precision
0.777, recall 0.806, F1 0.791), remains acceptable in the medium scenario
(precision 0.601, recall 0.586, F1 0.593), and deteriorates in the deliberately
adverse hard scenario (precision 0.318, recall 0.302, F1 0.310). This result is
important substantively because it quantifies both the usefulness and the
limits of the linking approach under realistic noise.

\paragraph{How it is used in the pipeline.}
Operationally, the benchmark is used to score a full threshold grid and then to
compare candidate broad, balanced, and strict rules before re-applying them to
real BOAMP candidate pairs. The balanced rule was retained because it preserved
acceptable synthetic reliability, especially in the medium scenario, while also
keeping enough real proxy events for downstream survival modeling. The benchmark
therefore acts as the calibration layer between the raw linking algorithm and
the final survival dataset: it informs the event-definition rule, while the real
BOAMP outputs remain the basis for event counts, Kaplan--Meier estimation,
Cox/AFT modeling, and operational risk indicators. The detailed implementation
is reproducible from \texttt{notebooks/04\_synthetic\_boamp\_benchmark.ipynb}
and \texttt{notebooks/05\_parameter\_calibration\_benchmark\_and\_real.ipynb},
with generated artifacts tracked under \texttt{data/synthetic/} and
\texttt{reports/tables/validation/}.

All three use $W=6$ and the corrected generic-CPV rule, where generic CPV codes

\texttt{data/processed/boamp\_phase2\_survival\_calibrated\_balanced.csv}

non-positive survival durations, no duplicate contract IDs, and no event without
a renewal candidate ID; the strict rule is flagged \texttt{LOW\_EVENTS}
(79 events $<$ 100) for Cox/AFT stability.

Under the balanced rule the Kaplan--Meier median is not reached; survival is
95.9\% at 12 months, 92.3\% at 24 months, 83.2\% at 48 months, and 75.2\% at
60 months. The Cox C-index is 0.592, and LogNormalAFT is the best balanced-rule
AFT model by AIC (3{,}544.8, ahead of Weibull 3{,}571.8 and LogLogistic
3{,}580.5). Re-scored 12/24-month risk indicators under this rule
(Section~\ref{sec:operational-indicators}) give mean probabilities of
0.023/0.068 and 27.5 expected recurrences within 12 months. These are
diagnostics for identifiable proxy recurrences in BOAMP, not estimates of
legally verified renewal probability.

\paragraph{Limitation.}
Because BOAMP does not provide an official renewal label, the constructed
\texttt{event} variable --- under any of the three calibrated rules, and under
the pre-calibration baseline --- should be read as a proxy for likely
procurement recurrence, not as verified legal renewal ground truth. The
synthetic benchmark quantifies how the linking method behaves under controlled,
known noise; it does not certify that any specific real BOAMP link is a true
renewal, and the manual audit above shows precision on real data is far lower
than on the easy/medium synthetic scenarios.

\paragraph{Conclusion of the calibration.}
The calibration phase's main deliverable is a change of objective, not a single
statistic: the pipeline moved from a rule that maximized \textbf{linkage
quantity} (665 events, 55.0\%, later found $\approx$9--15\% precise) to a rule
calibrated to maximize \textbf{analytical reliability} against a controlled
benchmark (269 events, 22.2\%). All downstream sections of this report should
be read with that distinction in mind: the original sections below describe the
pre-calibration baseline in full mechanical detail (still valid as a
description of the algorithm), while this update section gives the numbers to
actually use.

\section{Project Scope}

\subsection{Business Context}

Gigalis manages a portfolio of public digital infrastructure contracts in the
Pays-de-la-Loire region. A critical operational question is: \textit{when is a
procurement contract likely to be renewed?} Knowing this in advance allows
account managers to anticipate renewal campaigns, prepare competitive offers,
and reduce the risk of losing contracts by default.

\subsection{Why BOAMP?}

The \textbf{Bulletin Officiel des Annonces de March\'es Publics} (BOAMP) is the
official French public procurement gazette. All public contracts above the
relevant thresholds must be published there, making it the most complete source
of historical procurement data in France. It covers the full lifecycle:
pre-information, call for tender (\textit{appel d'offre}), award
(\textit{attribution}), and modification notices.

\subsection{Core Problem}

BOAMP does \textbf{not} provide an explicit field linking an original call for
tender to its renewal. Each notice is a standalone record. The renewal
relationship must therefore be \textbf{inferred} from a combination of signals:
the same buyer re-issuing a contract for a similar service in the expected
time-window after the original contract was due to expire.

\subsection{Scope of Phase 1}

Phase 1 produces a single BOAMP-only output table: one row per eligible
\textit{APPEL\_OFFRE} with a binary column \texttt{event}. In this report,
\texttt{event = 1} means that a plausible renewal was found by the linking
algorithm, while \texttt{event = 0} means that no renewal link was observed
under the chosen rule. The official Phase-2 handoff is
\texttt{boamp\_phase2\_survival.csv}, where the event definition and censoring
treatment are made explicit for survival modeling.

\subsection{Duration Field: Scientific Reliability Analysis}
\label{sec:duration_science}

\subsubsection*{Scientific Question}

\textit{Is the declared duration field (\texttt{duration\_clean}) reliable as
a survival target?}

The answer is \textbf{no}. The declared duration is preserved as a covariate
and as a temporal search prior for the linking algorithm, but it is not the
survival target. The true survival target --- the actual time elapsed between
a contract start and its renewal --- is the linked gap
$g(i, j^*) = t_{j^*} - t_i$, stored in \texttt{observed\_duration\_months}
in the output file.

\subsubsection*{Evidence}

Of the 1,933 \textit{APPEL\_OFFRE} notices, 1,480 (76.6\%) have a valid, non-suspect
\texttt{duration\_clean} value. Table~\ref{tab:duration_dist} shows the distribution
of these values.

\subsubsection*{Explanation}

French public procurement law (Code de la commande publique) requires the buyer
to declare the \textbf{administrative maximum duration} of the contract at the
time of publication. This is defined as the base period plus all possible
renewal tranches stacked end-to-end. A buyer writing a 12-month base contract
with three possible 12-month renewal options declares
\texttt{duration\_months = 48}. The 48 months is a legal ceiling approved at
signature, encoding the outer boundary of the contract, not a forecast of when
the buyer intends to re-enter the market.

Using this field as the survival target would mean training the model on
administrative ceilings rather than on observed behaviour. An AO that declares
48 months but renews after 12 months (at the end of the first base period)
would be incorrectly labelled as a 48-month contract. The error is not random
noise: it is systematically biased upward, because buyers default to the
maximum configured option when completing the publication form.

\subsubsection*{Resolution: What the Field Is Used For}

The declared duration $d_i$ is retained for three specific purposes:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Search prior in the linking algorithm.} The estimated end date
        $\hat{e}_i = t_i + d_i$ defines the centre of the temporal candidate
        window (Section~\ref{sec:duration_science} and Step 2 of the algorithm
        in Section~5). The declared duration tells the algorithm
        \textit{where to look} for a renewal notice, not \textit{what the
        answer is}.

  \item \textbf{Covariate in survival models.} The administrative duration
        choice encodes the contractual structure the buyer selected (single-year
        vs.\ multi-year framework). This is predictive of renewal behaviour
        even when the raw value does not equal the actual time-to-renewal.

  \item \textbf{Right-censoring indicator.} Contracts for which
        $\hat{e}_i = t_i + d_i > T_{\max}$ (December 2024) are excluded from
        the eligible denominator: their renewal window is not observable in the
        study period, regardless of algorithm quality.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent The \textbf{actual survival target} is the observed gap:

\begin{equation}
  T_i^{\text{obs}} = g(i, j^*) = t_{j^*} - t_i \quad \text{(months)}
\end{equation}

stored in \texttt{observed\_duration\_months} in \texttt{boamp\_renewal\_links.csv}.
This quantity is available only for the 665 linked contracts (\texttt{event = 1})
and is the correct input duration for the Cox and Kaplan--Meier models in
Phase 2. For right-censored contracts (\texttt{event = 0}), the survival
model requires a censoring time, which is typically taken as the study end
date minus the contract start date.

\section{Dataset Construction and Missing-Value Control}

This section explains the dataset in operational terms: what each row means,
why each intermediate table exists, how the modeling population is constructed,
and how incomplete fields are controlled. The objective is not only to document
the pipeline, but also to make the dataset explainable during a technical
discussion.

\subsection{What Each Dataset Represents}

The project does not start from a ready-made table of contract renewals. BOAMP
publishes \textbf{notices}, not longitudinal contract histories. A BOAMP row is
one publication event: a call for tenders, an award notice, a correction, or
another administrative notice. The analysis therefore transforms a notice-level
source into a contract-level survival dataset.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Raw notice corpus.} The API extraction and flattening step
        produces \nolinkurl{boamp_full_flat.csv} with 3,181 BOAMP notices and 30
        raw/flattened columns.
  \item \textbf{Cleaned analytical corpus.} The cleaning step produces
        \texttt{boamp\_full\_clean.csv} with 3,181 notices and 44 columns. This
        file keeps the raw variables and adds controlled fields such as
        \texttt{buyer\_key}, \texttt{cpv\_clean}, \texttt{amount\_clean},
        \texttt{duration\_clean}, and taxonomy labels.
  \item \textbf{Source contract population.} Only the 1,933
        \texttt{APPEL\_OFFRE} notices are used as possible source contracts,
        because they represent tenders that can later be re-tendered. Award,
        correction, and modification notices are informative context but are not
        survival units.
  \item \textbf{Eligible modeling population.} A source contract enters the
        final linking denominator only if its expected renewal window is
        observable before the study end date, 2024-12-31 (with the $W=6$-month
        search window, this requires an estimated end date on or before
        2024-06-30). This gives 1,210 eligible contracts; the other 723 source
        notices are too recent to be evaluated fairly.
  \item \textbf{Survival handoff.} The final file,
        \nolinkurl{boamp_phase2_survival.csv}, has one row per eligible contract,
        with \texttt{event}, \texttt{observed\_duration\_months}, renewal
        diagnostics, and covariates used by Phase 2.
\end{enumerate}

This distinction is important. The 3,181 rows describe the available BOAMP
evidence; the 1,933 \texttt{APPEL\_OFFRE} rows describe possible source
contracts; the 1,210-row handoff is the population on which the official
renewal-linking and survival results are measured.

\paragraph{Worked censoring example.}

follow-up, or the renewal was not detected.'' This is why the row remains in
Kaplan--Meier and Cox modeling.

\subsection{Why Right-Censoring Is Not Missing Data}

The final dataset contains 665 linked renewals and 545 unlinked eligible
contracts. The unlinked rows remain in the survival dataset because they still
carry valid information: they survived without an observed renewal until the
last date we could observe them. In survival-analysis notation, those rows have
\texttt{event = 0} and a censoring duration. Removing them would bias the model
toward contracts that renewed quickly and would make long-running or late-window
contracts disappear from the risk set.

The interpretation is therefore:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{event = 1}: the algorithm found a plausible later renewal
        notice; \nolinkurl{renewal_duration_months} is observed.
  \item \texttt{event = 0}: no accepted renewal was observed before study end;
        \nolinkurl{censoring_duration_months} is observed.
  \item \nolinkurl{observed_duration_months}: always filled, using the renewal
        gap for events and the time-to-study-end for censored rows.
\end{itemize}

This is why the 1,210-row handoff is suitable for Kaplan--Meier and Cox models:
every row contributes time-at-risk, even when the renewal event itself was not
observed.

\subsection{Practical Explanation of the Pipeline}

In plain terms, the dataset is built as follows. First, the project collects all
relevant digital-procurement BOAMP notices in Pays de la Loire from 2015 to
2024. Second, it cleans the unstable fields without overwriting the originals:
buyer names become canonical keys, CPV codes become comparable groups, amounts
and durations are flagged when implausible, and each contract receives a
technology category. Third, it keeps only call-for-tender notices as contracts
that can later be renewed. Fourth, it searches forward in time for later notices
from the same buyer that look like the same service being re-tendered. Finally,
it writes a survival table where each contract has a start date, a duration, an
event indicator, and enough diagnostics to judge whether the inferred renewal
link is strong or weak.

The most important limitation to remember is that the target variable is
constructed, not directly observed. The strength of the project is that this
construction is explicit: each assumption is visible in the output columns and
can be audited, adjusted, or tested through sensitivity analysis.

\section{Preprocessing Pipeline}

\subsection{Buyer Key Construction and SIREN Enrichment}
\label{sec:siren_enrichment}

\begin{equation}
  k_i^{(0)} =
  \begin{cases}
    \texttt{SIRET:} r_i & \text{if } r_i \in \{0,\ldots,9\}^{14} \\
    \texttt{NAME:} \phi(n_i) & \text{otherwise}
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}

For notice $i$, $n_i$ is the raw BOAMP buyer name
(\texttt{nomacheteur}), $r_i$ is the raw BOAMP buyer SIRET
(\texttt{buyer\_siret}), and $k_i^{(0)}$ is the initial buyer key used before
SIREN enrichment. SIRET is the 14-digit establishment identifier; SIREN is the
9-digit legal-entity identifier. BOAMP SIRET coverage is sparse, so the initial
rule is deliberately SIRET-first and name-fallback: use a precise establishment
identifier when it is valid, otherwise keep the row by grouping it under a
normalised name. The normalisation function $\phi(\cdot)$ lowercases, strips
accents, removes punctuation, removes legal or administrative suffixes when
matched by the local rule set, and collapses whitespace.

\paragraph{Unique-buyer table.}
The enrichment pipeline does not query the API once per notice. It first
constructs one row per distinct \texttt{nomacheteur}. Let
$\mathcal{U}$ be the set of unique raw buyer names and let
$I(u)=\{i:n_i=u\}$ be the notices carrying buyer name $u$. For each $u$, the
pipeline stores:

\begin{equation}
  x_u =
  \bigl(
    u,\; \phi(u),\; \operatorname{mode}\{k_i^{(0)}:i\in I(u)\},\;
    \delta_u,\; v_u,\; p_u,\; |I(u)|,\; |\{i\in I(u):\text{AO}\}|
  \bigr),
\end{equation}

\noindent where $\delta_u$ is the modal department code, $v_u$ the modal city,
and $p_u$ the modal postal code. Existing BOAMP SIRET values are preserved as
audit metadata but do not by themselves create a new SIREN key. This produces
525 unique buyer rows from the 3{,}181-notice corpus.

\paragraph{API candidate retrieval.}
For each $u\in\mathcal{U}$, the code detects a public-entity type
$a_u$ from name patterns such as \texttt{COMMUNE}, \texttt{SDIS}, \texttt{CHU},
\texttt{REGION}, \texttt{EPCI}, or \texttt{DEFAULT}. This determines both a
short query string $q_u$ and, when known, a French legal-nature filter
$\eta_u$ (\texttt{nature\_juridique}). The API search is attempted in a
controlled sequence:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item query $q_u$ with department $\delta_u$ and legal-nature filter $\eta_u$
        (except SDIS, where the legal-nature filter is skipped because it
        returned no results in this API);
  \item if no candidates are returned, query $q_u$ with the department only;
  \item if still empty, query $q_u$ with the legal-nature filter only;
  \item if still empty and the raw normalised name differs from $q_u$, query
        the raw normalised name with the department only.
\end{enumerate}

The API returns at most 10 active-entity candidates per buyer. Each candidate
$c_{ur}$ has rank $r$, SIREN $\sigma_{ur}$, establishment SIRET $\tau_{ur}$,
official name $m_{ur}$, NAF activity code, legal-nature code, city, and postal
code. All returned candidates are written to
\nolinkurl{boamp_buyer_siren_candidates.csv}; in the current run this table has
1{,}514 real API candidate rows for 401 of the 525 buyer names. Results are
cached by normalised buyer name and department so the pipeline is reproducible
without repeatedly calling the API.

\paragraph{Candidate scoring.}
The candidate score is a name-resolution score, not a procurement-renewal score.
Let $\psi(\cdot)$ denote the SIREN scoring normalisation: accents, case,
punctuation, legal suffixes, and common French stopwords are removed. Let
$h_u$ be the comparison string: for most buyers $h_u=\psi(u)$, but special
forms are rewritten to match the SIRENE naming convention, for example
\texttt{Ville de X} becomes \texttt{commune X} and SDIS is compared to
\texttt{sce departemental incendie secours sdis}. For candidate $c_{ur}$,

\begin{align}
  R(u,r) &= \operatorname{token\_sort\_ratio}\bigl(h_u,\psi(m_{ur})\bigr), \\
  O(u,r) &=
    \begin{cases}
      \dfrac{|\operatorname{Tok}(h_u)\cap \operatorname{Tok}(\psi(m_{ur}))|}
            {|\operatorname{Tok}(h_u)|}
      & \text{if } |\operatorname{Tok}(h_u)|>0,\\[6pt]
      0 & \text{otherwise},
    \end{cases} \\
  E(u,r) &= \min\{100,\;R(u,r)+8O(u,r)\}.
\end{align}

\noindent $R(u,r)$ is RapidFuzz token-sort similarity on $[0,100]$, $O(u,r)$ is
the fraction of query tokens recovered in the official candidate name, and
$E(u,r)$ is the stored \texttt{enrichment\_score}. The API rank is preserved:
the proposed match is the first returned API candidate, and the score and
margin are used as quality filters. If $n_u$ candidates are returned, define

\begin{equation}
  e_u^{(1)} = E(u,1), \qquad
  e_u^{(2)} =
  \begin{cases}
    E(u,2) & \text{if } n_u \geq 2,\\
    \text{NaN} & \text{if } n_u = 1,
  \end{cases}
  \qquad
  \Delta_u = e_u^{(1)} - e_u^{(2)}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Confidence classification and key upgrade.}
Confidence is assigned at the unique-buyer level:

\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{conf}(u)=
  \begin{cases}
    \text{HIGH} & e_u^{(1)} \geq 85,\\
    \text{HIGH} & e_u^{(1)} \geq 75 \;\land\; n_u=1,\\
    \text{HIGH} & e_u^{(1)} \geq 75 \;\land\; \Delta_u \geq 10,\\
    \text{MEDIUM} & e_u^{(1)} \geq 70 \text{ and not HIGH},\\
    \text{LOW} & e_u^{(1)} \geq 55 \text{ and not MEDIUM},\\
    \text{NO\_MATCH} & \text{otherwise.}
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}

Only HIGH-confidence matches with a valid SIREN change the grouping key:

\begin{equation}
  k_u^{(1)} =
  \begin{cases}
    \texttt{SIREN:}\sigma_{u1}
      & \text{if } \operatorname{conf}(u)=\text{HIGH}
        \text{ and } \sigma_{u1} \text{ exists},\\
    k_u^{(0)} & \text{otherwise.}
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}

\noindent Thus MEDIUM and LOW matches are diagnostic only; they are not allowed
to merge buyer groups. Buyers already keyed by BOAMP SIRET are also left
auditable as SIRET-origin cases. The enriched key $k_u^{(1)}$ is left-joined
back to all BOAMP notices by exact \texttt{nomacheteur}, preserving row count.
In the current run, 275 of 525 unique buyer names are HIGH confidence
(52.4\%), 21 MEDIUM, 32 LOW, and 197 NO\_MATCH. These HIGH matches create 275
\texttt{SIREN:} upgrades and merge 23 previously fragmented buyer groups. In
the final 1{,}210-row survival handoff, 724 rows use a \texttt{SIREN:} key,
484 retain a \texttt{NAME:} key, and 2 retain a \texttt{SIRET:} key.

\paragraph{Use in renewal linking.}
After enrichment, the active buyer identifier in the renewal-linking algorithm
is

\begin{equation}
  b_i = k_{\;n_i}^{(1)}.
\end{equation}

\noindent The matching logic is otherwise unchanged: SIREN enrichment changes
the blocking key, not the text, CPV, temporal, or composite-score formulas.
This is important for interpretation. A SIREN merge can add renewal candidates
because two spelling variants now share the same legal-entity key, but a
HIGH-confidence SIREN match is still automated entity resolution, not manual
ground truth.

\subsection{CPV Normalisation and Taxonomy Tagging}
\label{sec:taxonomy_method}

The taxonomy step converts heterogeneous CPV and text descriptions into a
single ICT segment label used in descriptive tables, bias checks, and survival
covariates. Let $x_i$ be the raw primary CPV value and $o_i$ the BOAMP
\textit{objet}. The CPV cleaning function $\kappa(\cdot)$ is:

\begin{equation}
  c_i = \kappa(x_i),
\end{equation}

\noindent where $\kappa$ drops the CPV check digit after a dash, removes
numeric-format artefacts such as \texttt{.0}, keeps the first 8 digits, restores
a lost leading zero for 7-digit numeric storage, and returns missing when no
usable digits remain. From the cleaned 8-digit code $c_i$, the pipeline derives

\begin{equation}
  c_i^{(2)}=c_i[1\!:\!2],\quad
  c_i^{(3)}=c_i[1\!:\!3],\quad
  c_i^{(4)}=c_i[1\!:\!4],\quad
  c_i^{(5)}=c_i[1\!:\!5],\quad
  c_i^{(8)}=c_i,
\end{equation}

\noindent stored respectively as \texttt{cpv\_div2}, \texttt{cpv\_group3},
\texttt{cpv\_class4}, \texttt{cpv\_category5}, and \texttt{cpv\_full8}. A code
ending in \texttt{000000} is flagged as generic because it identifies only a
broad division, not a specific service.

The taxonomy file \nolinkurl{data/processed/taxonomy.csv} defines 10 ICT
categories. For category $q$, let $P_q$ be its set of CPV prefixes and $K_q$
its keyword regular expression. The assignment is CPV-first:

\begin{equation}
  L(i,q) =
  \max_{p\in P_q}
  \Bigl\{|p| : c_i \text{ starts with } p\Bigr\},
  \qquad
  L(i,q)=0 \text{ if no prefix matches.}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
  q_i^{\text{cpv}} =
  \operatorname*{arg\,max}_{q} L(i,q)
  \quad \text{if } \max_q L(i,q)>0.
\end{equation}

\noindent The longest matching prefix wins. If two categories would tie at the
same prefix length, the first category order in \texttt{taxonomy.csv} is kept,
because the implementation updates the best match only when a strictly longer
prefix is found. If no CPV prefix matches, the pipeline falls back to the
contract description:

\begin{equation}
  q_i^{\text{text}} =
  \min_{\text{taxonomy order}}
  \left\{q : K_q(o_i)=1\right\}.
\end{equation}

The final category is therefore:

\begin{equation}
  q_i =
  \begin{cases}
    q_i^{\text{cpv}} & \text{if } \max_q L(i,q)>0,\\
    q_i^{\text{text}} & \text{if no CPV prefix matches and a keyword matches},\\
    \text{CAT\_UNKNOWN} & \text{otherwise.}
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}

\noindent This rule is intentionally conservative: structured CPV evidence has
priority, keyword matching only rescues rows without a usable CPV-prefix match,
and no row is forced into a digital category when both signals are absent. In
\texttt{boamp\_full\_clean.csv}, CPV is cleaned for 3{,}119 of 3{,}181 notices
(98.1\%). The keyword fallback assigns a non-Unknown category to 42 notices
whose cleaned CPV is missing; the remaining unresolved notices stay in the
Unknown bucket.

\subsection{Contract Start Date Refinement}

Award date from linked ATTRIBUTION notices is used when available, falling
back to the publication date:

\begin{equation}
  t_i =
  \begin{cases}
    \text{award\_date}(j) & \text{if } \exists\, j \in \text{ATTR} : \texttt{annonce\_lie}(j) = i \\
    \texttt{dateparution}_i & \text{otherwise}
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}

In practice, 40.7\% of eligible AO obtain a more precise start date through
this refinement.

\subsection{Duration Imputation}

\begin{equation}
  d_i =
  \begin{cases}
    \texttt{duration\_clean}_i & \text{if not missing and not suspect} \\
    d_0 = 48 \text{ months}    & \text{otherwise (23.4\% of AO)}
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Estimated End Date}

\begin{equation}
  \hat{e}_i = t_i + d_i
\end{equation}

\subsection{Text Normalisation}

The \textit{objet} field is normalised for TF-IDF (used in calibration) and
passed raw to Sentence-Transformers:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Lowercase
  \item Strip Unicode accents (NFD normalisation)
  \item Keep only characters matching \texttt{[a-z0-9 ]}
  \item Collapse multiple spaces
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Sentence-Transformer Embeddings}

Each normalised \textit{objet} $\mathbf{o}_i$ is encoded into a 384-dimensional
unit-norm vector:

\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{v}_i = \text{ST}\bigl(\mathbf{o}_i\bigr) \in \mathbb{R}^{384},
  \qquad \|\mathbf{v}_i\|_2 = 1
\end{equation}

Model used: \texttt{paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2}
(multilingual, 384 dimensions, $\sim$120 MB).

\section{Renewal Linking Algorithm}

\subsection{Notation}

Let $\mathcal{A}$ denote the set of all \textit{APPEL\_OFFRE} notices. Each
notice $i \in \mathcal{A}$ is represented by:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item $t_i$: contract start date, defined as the award date when available,
        otherwise the BOAMP publication date;
  \item $d_i$: contract duration in months, after cleaning and imputation;
  \item $\hat{e}_i = t_i + d_i$: estimated contract end date;
  \item $\text{CPV}_i$: main CPV classification code;
  \item $\mathbf{o}_i$: contract description text (\textit{objet});
  \item $b_i$: buyer identifier, using SIRET when available and normalised
        buyer name otherwise.
\end{itemize}

The goal is to construct, for each eligible source notice $i$, an algorithmic
binary outcome $Y_i$ indicating whether a plausible renewal candidate can be found among later
BOAMP calls-for-tender.

\subsection{Step 1 --- Eligibility Filter}

A notice is \textbf{eligible} to be a renewal source if its full renewal search
window (estimated end date $\pm W$ months, $W=6$) closes within the study
period $[T_{\min}, T_{\max}]$:

\begin{equation}
  i \in \mathcal{A}_{\text{elig}}
  \iff
  \hat{e}_i + W \leq T_{\max}
  \quad
  \bigl(T_{\min} = \text{2015-01-01},\; T_{\max} = \text{2024-12-31}\bigr),
\end{equation}

\noindent i.e.\ the estimated end date must fall on or before 2024-06-30.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Eligibility breakdown}
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
Population & Count \\
\midrule
Total APPEL\_OFFRE              & 1,933 \\
Not eligible: renewal window closes after 2024-12-31 & 723 \\
\textbf{Eligible} ($\mathcal{A}_{\text{elig}}$) & \textbf{1,210} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Contracts with $\hat{e}_i > T_{\max}$ are excluded from the Phase 1 linking
denominator because the renewal opportunity is not sufficiently observable in
the 2015--2024 dataset.

\subsection{Step 2 --- Candidate Pool}

For each eligible source $i$, the candidate pool is:

\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{C}(i) = \bigl\{
    j \in \mathcal{A} :
    b_j = b_i,\quad
    t_j > t_i,\quad
    |t_j - \hat{e}_i| \leq W
  \bigr\},
  \quad W = 6 \text{ months}
\end{equation}

The gap between source and candidate is:

\begin{equation}
  g(i,j) = t_j - t_i \quad \text{(in months)}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Key design choice:} candidates are grouped by \texttt{buyer\_key} only.
The previous baseline grouped by \texttt{[buyer\_key, cpv\_div2]}, which
rejected valid renewals where the buyer reclassified the service under a
different CPV code.

\subsection{Step 3 --- Component Scores}

\paragraph{Text similarity.}
Cosine similarity between unit-norm embeddings (dot product = cosine when
both vectors are $\ell_2$-normalised):

\begin{equation}
  s_{\text{text}}(i,j) = \mathbf{v}_i \cdot \mathbf{v}_j
\end{equation}

\noindent In theory, cosine similarity lies in $[-1,1]$. In this application,
the hard text-similarity filter below retains only candidate pairs with
$s_{\text{text}}(i,j) \geq 0.20$.

\paragraph{CPV compatibility.}
A hierarchy-based score following the official CPV nomenclature, in which a code
is read left-to-right as division (2 digits), group (3), class (4), category (5)
and full code (8); a 9th digit after a dash is only a check digit. The score
rewards the depth of the longest shared prefix. A CPV code is treated as generic
if it ends in \texttt{000000} (e.g. \texttt{72000000} for IT services in
general); such codes carry no sub-division signal and are capped at the division
level. A missing CPV is \emph{excluded} from the composite (returned as
\texttt{NaN}) rather than scored, and the remaining weights are renormalized
(Section~\ref{sec:composite}).

\begin{equation}
  s_{\text{cpv}}(i,j) =
  \begin{cases}
    \text{NaN} & \text{either CPV missing (excluded, renormalized)} \\
    0.20 & \text{either generic \& same division} \\
    0.00 & \text{either generic \& different division} \\
    1.00 & \text{CPV}[:8]_i = \text{CPV}[:8]_j \text{ (same full code)} \\
    0.80 & \text{CPV}[:5]_i = \text{CPV}[:5]_j \text{ (same category)} \\
    0.60 & \text{CPV}[:4]_i = \text{CPV}[:4]_j \text{ (same class)} \\
    0.40 & \text{CPV}[:3]_i = \text{CPV}[:3]_j \text{ (same group)} \\
    0.20 & \text{CPV}[:2]_i = \text{CPV}[:2]_j \text{ (same division)} \\
    0.00 & \text{otherwise (valid but different division)}
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Temporal proximity.}
Linear decay from 1.0 at the exact expected renewal date to 0.0 at the
window boundary $W = 6$ months:

\begin{equation}
  s_{\text{temp}}(i,j) = \max\!\left(0,\; 1 - \frac{|g(i,j) - d_i|}{W}\right)
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Buyer match.}
Always 1.0 within the same-buyer grouping loop:

\begin{equation}
  s_{\text{buyer}}(i,j) = 1.0
\end{equation}

\noindent Buyer identity is therefore mainly a \emph{blocking rule}: candidates
are first restricted to the same buyer group. The constant buyer score does not
change the ranking among candidates for the same source contract, but it is kept
explicit to document the full matching design and to allow future extension to
cross-buyer matching.

\subsection{Step 4 --- Composite Score}
\label{sec:composite}

When the CPV code is present on both notices, the four components are combined
with fixed weights:

\begin{equation}
  \boxed{
    S(i,j) =
    0.40\cdot s_{\text{text}} +
    0.25\cdot s_{\text{cpv}} +
    0.20\cdot s_{\text{temp}} +
    0.15\cdot s_{\text{buyer}}
  }
\end{equation}

When the CPV code is missing on either notice, CPV is excluded and the remaining
component weights are renormalized by their sum
($0.40+0.20+0.15 = 0.75$) so the score stays in $[0,1]$:

\begin{equation}
  S(i,j) =
  \frac{0.40\cdot s_{\text{text}} + 0.20\cdot s_{\text{temp}} + 0.15\cdot s_{\text{buyer}}}{0.75}.
\end{equation}

Missing CPV is therefore never assigned an arbitrary neutral $0.5$. A boolean
\nolinkurl{cpv_used_in_score} column records, per pair, whether CPV contributed.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Weight rationale}
\begin{tabular}{@{}P{2.5cm}R{1.4cm}P{9.0cm}@{}}
\toprule
Component & Weight & Rationale \\
\midrule
$s_{\text{text}}$   & 0.40 & Strongest discriminator. Semantic similarity captures service identity beyond
                              lexical overlap. Calibration shows ST cosine $\geq$ 0.30 for 96\% of verified
                              same-contract pairs. \\[4pt]
$s_{\text{cpv}}$    & 0.25 & Structured and reliable when specific. Degrades gracefully through the CPV
                              hierarchy (full code $\to$ category $\to$ class $\to$ group $\to$ division);
                              generic codes capped at the division level (0.20). Missing CPV is excluded
                              and the remaining weights renormalized. Not used as a hard gate because
                              buyers sometimes reclassify. \\[4pt]
$s_{\text{temp}}$   & 0.20 & Rewards timing alignment without hard-excluding distant candidates
                              (hard filters apply separately). \\[4pt]
$s_{\text{buyer}}$  & 0.15 & Constant within the loop; kept explicit so all weights sum to 1.0.
                              Included for future extension to cross-buyer matching. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\subsection{Step 5 --- Hard Filters}

Two hard gates are applied before best-match selection:

\begin{align}
  s_{\text{text}}(i,j) &\geq 0.20 \label{eq:f1} \\
  g(i,j) &\in [6,\; 72] \text{ months} \label{eq:f2}
\end{align}

The minimum gap of 6 months prevents matching a contract to itself or to an
immediate amendment. The maximum gap of 72 months (6 years) covers contracts
with the longest expected durations.

\noindent CPV is intentionally \textbf{not} used as a hard filter. Requiring CPV
agreement as a gate would exclude legitimate renewals when the same buyer
reclassifies a similar service under a different CPV code.

Let $\mathcal{F}(i)$ denote the filtered candidate set:

\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{F}(i) =
  \bigl\{
    j \in \mathcal{C}(i) :
    s_{\text{text}}(i,j) \geq 0.20,\;
    g(i,j) \in [6,72]
  \bigr\}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Step 6 --- Best-Match Selection}

For each source, keep the single highest-scoring candidate:

\begin{equation}
  j^*(i) = \operatorname*{arg\,max}_{j \in \mathcal{F}(i)}\; S(i,j)
\end{equation}

where $\mathcal{F}(i) \subseteq \mathcal{C}(i)$ is the subset passing both
hard filters~(\ref{eq:f1}) and (\ref{eq:f2}).

\subsection{Step 7 --- Outcome Variable}

The Phase 1 binary outcome is an \emph{algorithmic linking outcome}. It records
whether the matching rule found a plausible renewal candidate for source
contract $i$.

\begin{equation}
  Y_i =
	  \begin{cases}
	    1 & \text{if } \mathcal{F}(i) \neq \emptyset \quad \text{(renewal found)} \\
	    0 & \text{if } \mathcal{F}(i) = \emptyset    \quad \text{(no renewal link observed)}
	  \end{cases}
\end{equation}

\noindent The final linking rate is:

\begin{equation}
  \text{Linking rate}
  =
  \frac{\sum_{i \in \mathcal{A}_{\text{elig}}} Y_i}
       {|\mathcal{A}_{\text{elig}}|}
  =
  \frac{665}{1{,}210}
  =
  55.0\%.
\end{equation}

The value $Y_i = 0$ should be interpreted as an unobserved link under the
available data and chosen thresholds, not as definitive evidence that no renewal
occurred.

Quantity & Description & Value \\
\midrule

$t_j$           & Candidate start date       & 2017-12-17 \\

\noindent This pair passes both hard filters ($s_{\text{text}} = 0.69 \geq 0.20$;

\textbf{Concrete step}

This part illustrates how the renewal-linking score is computed for one
concrete candidate pair. The example comes from the first row of
\texttt{boamp\_renewal\_candidates.csv}.

The source contract is:

\[
\texttt{src} = \texttt{15-31464}
\]

The candidate renewal notice is:

\[
\texttt{cand} = \texttt{17-176560}
\]

Both notices belong to the same buyer group:

\[
\text{Buyer} = \text{D\'epartement de la Mayenne}
\]

\subsection*{Source and Candidate Information}

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Source contract and candidate renewal}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Element & Notation & Value \\
\midrule
Source contract ID & $i$ & \texttt{15-31464} \\
Source start date & $t_i$ & 2015-03-02 \\
Declared duration & $d_i$ & 36 months \\
Estimated end date & $\hat{e}_i = t_i + d_i$ & 2018-03 \\
Candidate contract ID & $j$ & \texttt{17-176560} \\
Candidate start date & $t_j$ & 2017-12-17 \\
Buyer match & $b_i = b_j$ & Yes \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The source contract started on 2015-03-02 and had a declared duration of
36 months. Therefore, the expected renewal date is around March 2018:

\[
\hat{e}_i = 2015\text{-}03\text{-}02 + 36 \text{ months}
\approx 2018\text{-}03
\]

The candidate notice was published on 2017-12-17. This is close to the expected
end date of the original contract, so it is a plausible renewal candidate.

\subsection*{Step 1: Time Gap}

The time gap between the source contract and the candidate is:

\[
g(i,j) = t_j - t_i
\]

In this example:

\[
g(i,j)
=
2017\text{-}12\text{-}17
-
2015\text{-}03\text{-}02
=
33.54 \text{ months}
\]

The declared duration was:

\[
d_i = 36 \text{ months}
\]

So the candidate appears:

\[
36 - 33.54 = 2.46 \text{ months}
\]

before the expected contract end date.

\subsection*{Step 2: Temporal Score}

The temporal score is:

\[
s_{\text{temp}}(i,j)
=
1 -
\frac{|g(i,j) - d_i|}{W}
\]

with:

\[
W = 6
\]

Therefore:

\[
s_{\text{temp}}(i,j)
=
1 -
\frac{|33.54 - 36|}{12}
\]

\[
s_{\text{temp}}(i,j)
=
1 -
\frac{2.46}{12}
\]

\[
s_{\text{temp}}(i,j)
=
0.7951
\]

This is a high temporal score because the candidate appears only about
2.5 months before the expected renewal date.

\subsection*{Step 3: Other Component Scores}

The text similarity score is computed using the cosine similarity between the
two Sentence-Transformer embeddings:

\[
s_{\text{text}}(i,j) = 0.6922
\]

This means that the two contract descriptions are semantically close.

The CPV score is:

\[
s_{\text{cpv}}(i,j) = \text{NaN (excluded)}
\]

because the source CPV code is missing. Instead of inventing a neutral value, the
algorithm \emph{excludes} CPV from the composite and renormalizes the remaining
component weights. This is important because missing CPV should neither count as
evidence against the match nor inject an arbitrary $0.5$ signal.

The buyer score is:

\[
s_{\text{buyer}}(i,j) = 1.0
\]

because the two notices belong to the same buyer group.

\subsection*{Step 4: Composite Score}

Because the source CPV is missing, CPV is excluded and the composite is the
renormalized weighted sum of the three available components:

\[
S(i,j)
=
\frac{0.40s_{\text{text}}
+
0.20s_{\text{temp}}
+
0.15s_{\text{buyer}}}{0.40 + 0.20 + 0.15}
\]

Substituting the values:

\[
S(i,j)
=
\frac{0.40(0.6922)
+
0.20(0.7951)
+
0.15(1.0)}{0.75}
\]

\[
S(i,j)
=
\frac{0.2769
+
0.1590
+
0.1500}{0.75}
=
\frac{0.5859}{0.75}
\]

\[
S(i,j)
=
0.7812
\]

So the final composite score is:

\[
\boxed{S(i,j) = 0.7812}
\]

This matches the \texttt{composite\_score} reported in the candidate file.

\subsection*{Interpretation}

This pair is considered a plausible renewal link because all main signals are
consistent:

\begin{itemize}
  \item the buyer is the same;
  \item the candidate appears close to the expected contract end date;
  \item the contract descriptions are semantically similar;
  \item the CPV code is missing for the source, but this is excluded from the
        composite (weights renormalized) rather than treated as evidence against
        the match or scored a neutral $0.5$.
\end{itemize}

The pair also passes the two hard filters:

\[
s_{\text{text}} = 0.6922 \geq 0.20
\]

and:

\[
g(i,j) = 33.54 \in [6,72]
\]

Therefore, this candidate remains in the filtered candidate set. If it has the
highest composite score among all surviving candidates for source
\texttt{15-31464}, it is selected as the best renewal match:

\[
j^*(i) = \texttt{17-176560}
\]

This example shows exactly how the algorithm transforms raw BOAMP notices into
a renewal-linking score. The score is not legal proof of renewal. It is an
algorithmic measure of plausibility based on buyer identity, timing, text
similarity, and CPV compatibility.

\section{Limitations and Risks}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, label=\textbf{L\arabic*.}]

  \item \textbf{No explicit ground truth.} BOAMP does not provide a renewal
        field. The \texttt{annonce\_lie} signal on ATTRIBUTION notices
        calibrates text similarity thresholds but is not a direct renewal label.
        A manual audit of a stratified 150-case sample (2026-07-02; see
        Section~\ref{sec:manual_audit}) estimates the precision of the 665
        links at only $\approx$9--15\% overall, rising to $\approx$0.50 in the
        HIGH/strict tier and 1.00 at text similarity $\geq 0.80$.

  \item \textbf{Name-based buyer fragmentation.} Before SIREN enrichment, most buyer grouping was name-based. After enrichment, 724 of the 1,210 eligible contracts use a SIREN key (plus 2 SIRET), while 484 remain NAME-keyed. Residual buyer fragmentation therefore remains possible only for unresolved NAME-keyed buyers. Variant spellings of the same public entity (abbreviations,
        accents, word order) create separate groups, producing structural false
        negatives that cannot be recovered by tuning thresholds alone.

  \item \textbf{Duration imputation introduces noise, and declared duration is
        an administrative ceiling.} 24.4\% of eligible AO had their duration
        imputed to 48 months. If the true duration differs significantly, the
        temporal window is shifted and the correct renewal candidate may fall
        outside the $\pm$6-month window. More fundamentally, even when duration
        is not imputed, the declared value is the administrative maximum of the
        contract (base period plus all renewal tranches), not the expected actual
        first renewal date. It cannot therefore be used as the survival target.
        See Section~\ref{sec:duration_science} for the full analysis.

  \item \textbf{False positives in sparse-buyer groups.} Relaxing CPV from a
        hard filter to a scored component increases recall but also increases the
        risk of linking two unrelated contracts from the same buyer that happen to
        use similar procurement language (e.g.\ generic IT services descriptions).

  \item \textbf{Sample scope.} The dataset covers only the Pays-de-la-Loire
        region and is focused on ICT/digital services. Linking rates and score
        distributions may differ significantly for other regions or sectors.

\end{enumerate}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Model A --- Broad baseline}: all 665 events (text $\geq 0.20$, all accepted links; no composite floor).
  \item \textbf{Model B --- Sensitivity check}: 400 events only (drop LOW tier, i.e.\ composite $\geq 0.50$).

$\geq 0.50$, composite score $\geq 0.50$, $W=6$, corrected generic CPV

survival input, with broad and strict rules kept as sensitivity checks.

\subsection{Score Disambiguation: Margin Between Best and Second-Best Candidate}
\label{sec:margin}

For source contracts that had more than one candidate, the margin between the
best and second-best composite score measures how clearly the algorithm resolved
the ambiguity. A small margin (best $\approx$ second-best) indicates that an
alternative renewal link was nearly as plausible as the selected one.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.82\textwidth]{figures/fig_score_margin}
\caption{Distribution of the score margin $m_i$ (best composite $-$ second-best
composite) for the 501 multi-candidate source contracts. The dotted red line
marks the 0.05 threshold below which 43.7\% of pairs are considered
\textit{ambiguous}: the algorithm selected one candidate, but an alternative
was within 0.05 of the same score. The dashed green line at 0.10 marks the
\textit{clear winner} zone (30.3\% of pairs). These ambiguous pairs are
concentrated in the LOW and MEDIUM confidence tiers and are the primary
motivation for the manual validation sample recommended in Section~11.}
\label{fig:score_margin}
\end{figure}

The 43.7\% ambiguous-margin rate does not mean the algorithm chose wrongly in
those cases --- it means the available signals were not strong enough to
distinguish the best candidate from the runner-up with high certainty. This is
a structural property of the data (multiple contracts from the same buyer in
similar time windows) and cannot be resolved by tuning thresholds. Manual
validation of a stratified sample is the appropriate mitigation.

\paragraph{Operationalizing the margin: \texttt{high\_confidence\_strict}.}
We turn this diagnostic into an explicit, conservative flag, written to
\texttt{boamp\_renewal\_links.csv}, the Phase~2 handoff, and a dedicated
\texttt{boamp\_link\_confidence\_diagnostics.csv}. A link is \emph{strict
high-confidence} when it satisfies \emph{both} conditions
\[
  \texttt{event}=1 \;\wedge\; S(i,j^*) \geq 0.70 \;\wedge\;
  m_i \geq 0.05, \qquad m_i = S(i,j^{(1)}) - S(i,j^{(2)}),
\]
i.e.\ a high absolute score \emph{and} a clear separation from the runner-up.
Of the 665 links, \textbf{75} qualify. Single-candidate sources ($n = 164$)
have an undefined margin and are recorded as \texttt{single\_candidate\_match}
rather than auto-promoted to strict, so a ``clear winner among several
candidates'' is never conflated with an ``only candidate available''. This flag
is deliberately conservative: it is \emph{not} a new ground truth but a
sensitivity definition used to bound the renewal signal in Model~C
(Section~\ref{sec:reliability} protocol; results in
Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity}).

\subsection{Indirect External Calibration (\texttt{annonce\_lie})}

An indirect calibration is possible using the \texttt{annonce\_lie} field on
ATTRIBUTION notices. These notices back-reference the original call-for-tender,
providing external evidence of real procurement activity on those AO records.
Among the 466 eligible AO notices that are referenced by at least one
ATTRIBUTION notice, 318 were linked (68.2\%), compared to 55.0\% overall.
The algorithm performs \textit{better} on contracts with external evidence of
activity, which is the expected direction.

\subsection{Statistical Methods and Notation}
\label{sec:phase2_methods}

Let $Y_i$ denote the Phase 1 algorithmic linking outcome. In Phase 2, the
standard survival-analysis event indicator is defined as $\delta_i = Y_i$.
Thus $\delta_i = 1$ means that an identifiable BOAMP renewal was linked by the
algorithm, and $\delta_i = 0$ means that no renewal was observed under the
available data and matching rule.

Let $T_i$ denote the latent time-to-identifiable BOAMP renewal (in months) for contract $i$ and
$C_i$ the censoring time (time from contract start to end of study period for
right-censored observations). What is observed is the pair
$(T_i^{\text{obs}},\, \delta_i)$ where:

\begin{equation}
  T_i^{\text{obs}} = \min(T_i,\, C_i),
  \qquad
  \delta_i = \mathbf{1}[T_i \leq C_i].
\end{equation}

In the output file, $T_i^{\text{obs}}$ is stored in
\texttt{observed\_duration\_months} and $\delta_i$ in \texttt{event}.

\paragraph{Kaplan--Meier estimator.}
\textbf{Purpose.} The Kaplan--Meier (KM) estimator is used here to describe the
distribution of time until an \emph{identifiable BOAMP renewal} appears under
the linking rule, while correctly handling right-censored contracts.

Let $t_1 < t_2 < \cdots < t_k$ be the distinct observed event times, $d_j$ the
number of events at time $t_j$, and $n_j$ the number of contracts at risk just
before $t_j$. The product-limit estimator of the survival function is:

\begin{equation}
  \hat{S}(t) = \prod_{j:\, t_j \leq t} \left(1 - \frac{d_j}{n_j}\right).
\end{equation}

\textbf{How to read the result.} $\hat{S}(t)$ is the estimated probability
that a contract has \emph{not yet} shown an identifiable BOAMP renewal by time
$t$. The KM median is the time at which this estimated survival probability
falls to 0.5.

\textbf{Limitation.} The KM curve estimates timing under the constructed proxy
event variable. It does not prove the legal truth of renewal or non-renewal.

\paragraph{Log-rank test.}
\textbf{Purpose.} The log-rank test compares survival curves across groups,
such as duration bins or imputation status, to assess whether the observed
renewal-time distributions differ more than would be expected from random
variation alone.

For two strata, the log-rank statistic can be written as:

\begin{equation}
  \chi^2_{\text{LR}}
  =
  \frac{\left(\sum_j (O_{1j} - E_{1j})\right)^2}
       {\sum_j V_{1j}},
\end{equation}

where $O_{1j}$ is the observed and $E_{1j}$ the expected number of events in
stratum 1 at time $t_j$, and $V_{1j}$ is the hypergeometric variance. For
$K>2$ strata, the test generalises to a vector/matrix form and follows a
$\chi^2$ distribution with $K - 1$ degrees of freedom under the null hypothesis
of equal survival.

\textbf{How to read the result.} A small $p$-value means the data provide
evidence that at least one group has a different renewal-time distribution. A
non-significant result does \emph{not} prove equal curves; it means the data do
not provide strong evidence of a difference.

\textbf{Limitation.} The log-rank test is a hypothesis test, not an effect-size
measure, and it does not explain why curves differ.

\paragraph{Cox proportional hazards model.}
\textbf{Purpose.} The Cox model is used to estimate how observed covariates are
associated with the relative rate at which a proxy renewal event occurs,
without specifying the baseline hazard shape.

The hazard function for contract $i$ with covariate vector $\mathbf{x}_i$ is:

\begin{equation}
  h(t \mid \mathbf{x}_i)
  =
  h_0(t)\,\exp\!\bigl(\boldsymbol{\beta}^\top \mathbf{x}_i\bigr),
\end{equation}

where $h_0(t)$ is an unspecified baseline hazard. Parameters $\boldsymbol{\beta}$
are estimated by maximising the partial likelihood. For readability, the
no-ties form is:

\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\beta})
  =
  \prod_{i:\,\delta_i = 1}
  \frac{\exp\!\bigl(\boldsymbol{\beta}^\top \mathbf{x}_i\bigr)}
       {\displaystyle\sum_{j \in \mathcal{R}(T_i^{\text{obs}})}
        \exp\!\bigl(\boldsymbol{\beta}^\top \mathbf{x}_j\bigr)},
\end{equation}

where $\mathcal{R}(t) = \{j : T_j^{\text{obs}} \geq t\}$ is the risk set at
time $t$. The hazard ratio for covariate $k$ is $\mathrm{HR}_k = \exp(\beta_k)$;
a value $> 1$ indicates a higher instantaneous renewal rate (faster renewal).
Because the empirical durations are measured in months, tied event times can
occur; the software implementation handles ties internally.
All Cox and AFT models are fitted with a small ridge ($L_2$) penalty of
$\lambda = 0.1$ on the coefficients (the lifelines \texttt{penalizer}
parameter). This stabilises estimation in the presence of correlated dummy
covariates and mildly shrinks the reported hazard ratios and confidence
intervals toward the null; exact reproduction of the tabulated values requires
the same penalty.

\textbf{How to read the result.} A hazard ratio is a \emph{relative} rate.
For example, $\mathrm{HR}=1.10$ means a 10\% higher instantaneous renewal rate,
while $\mathrm{HR}=0.90$ means a 10\% lower instantaneous renewal rate,
conditional on the other covariates in the model.

\textbf{Limitation.} These are associations conditional on the constructed
proxy event variable. They should not be interpreted as causal effects.

\paragraph{Concordance index (C-index).}
\textbf{Purpose.} The C-index measures ranking ability: if two contracts are
compared, does the model assign higher risk to the contract that renews
earlier?

In-sample ranking ability is measured by Harrell's C-index:

\begin{equation}
  C
  =
  \frac{
    \displaystyle\sum_{(i,j):\, T_i^{\text{obs}} < T_j^{\text{obs}},\; \delta_i = 1}
    \mathbf{1}\bigl[\hat{\eta}_i > \hat{\eta}_j\bigr]
  }{
    \displaystyle\sum_{(i,j):\, T_i^{\text{obs}} < T_j^{\text{obs}},\; \delta_i = 1}
    1
  },
\end{equation}

where $\hat{\eta}_i = \boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}^\top \mathbf{x}_i$ is the
estimated linear predictor. $C = 0.5$ corresponds to random ranking;
$C = 1$ is perfect discrimination.

\textbf{How to read the result.} Values near 0.5 mean the model is little
better than random ordering; values near 1 mean near-perfect ordering. A value
around 0.65, as in this report, indicates moderate ranking signal.

\textbf{Limitation.} The reported C-index is in-sample. It is useful for
internal comparison, but it is not a full out-of-sample validation.

\paragraph{Accelerated failure time (AFT) models.}
\textbf{Purpose.} AFT models complement the Cox analysis by modeling the
logarithm of time-to-renewal directly. They are useful when one wants to reason
about survival time itself rather than only relative hazard.

Parametric AFT models assume:

\begin{equation}
  \log T_i = \mu + \boldsymbol{\gamma}^\top \mathbf{x}_i + \sigma \epsilon_i,
\end{equation}

where the distribution of $\epsilon_i$ determines the model family:

\begin{equation}
  \epsilon_i \sim
  \begin{cases}
    \mathcal{N}(0,1)  & \text{log-normal} \\
    \text{Logistic}(0,1) & \text{log-logistic} \\
    \text{Gumbel}(0,1) & \text{Weibull}
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}

Parameters $(\mu, \boldsymbol{\gamma}, \sigma)$ are estimated by maximum
likelihood with right-censored observations. Model selection uses the
Akaike Information Criterion:

\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{AIC} = -2\,\hat{\ell} + 2p,
\end{equation}

where $\hat{\ell}$ is the maximised log-likelihood and $p$ is the number of
free parameters.

\textbf{How to read the result.} Lower AIC means a better trade-off between
goodness-of-fit and complexity \emph{among models fitted on the same dataset}.

\textbf{Limitation.} AIC is not a hypothesis test, not a p-value, and not an
accuracy score.

\paragraph{Proportional hazards assumption.}
\textbf{Purpose.} The Cox model does \emph{not} assume that renewal risk is
constant over time. It assumes that the ratio of hazards between two contracts
or groups is approximately constant over time.

\begin{equation}
  \frac{h(t \mid \mathbf{x}_i)}{h(t \mid \mathbf{x}_j)}
  \quad \text{should not depend on } t.
\end{equation}

A graphical check uses the log-log transformation: if the PH assumption holds,
$\log(-\log\hat{S}_k(t))$ curves across strata $k$ should be approximately
parallel. The formal Schoenfeld residual test is a hypothesis test with
$H_0$: proportional hazards holds, and $H_1$: the proportional-hazards
assumption is violated.

\textbf{How to read the result.} A flagged covariate suggests that its Cox
hazard ratio should be interpreted as an average relative effect over the
observation window, not as a perfectly constant effect at every month.

\textbf{Limitation.} A violation of the PH assumption does not automatically
invalidate the whole analysis. It indicates that a time-varying or alternative
specification may be preferable for that covariate.

Following the internship guide's validation protocol, the Cox model was also

form the test set (116 rows, 60 events). The reduced Cox model refitted on the

so this figure is a directional check rather than a definitive out-of-time

\subsubsection*{Proportional Hazards Assumption Check}

The proportional hazards assumption was checked two ways: a formal Schoenfeld
residual test (lifelines \texttt{check\_assumptions}) and a graphical log-log
transformation of the KM survival function. The Schoenfeld test evaluates
$H_0$: the proportional-hazards assumption holds, against $H_1$: it is
violated. The test flags \texttt{declared\_duration\_months} ($p < 5\times10^{-5}$), the
strongest predictor in the model; the remaining covariates pass. This indicates
a time-varying (non-proportional) effect for declared duration. This does not
mean the renewal risk itself is constant over time; it means the relative
hazard ratio associated with declared duration is not perfectly constant over
time. We therefore interpret the reported Cox hazard ratio as an average
relative effect over the observation window rather than as a perfectly fixed
month-by-month multiplier. We interpret
this with caution rather than as invalidating: the Schoenfeld test is highly
sensitive to functional-form misspecification, and the likely cause here is a
mildly non-linear relationship between declared duration and the log-hazard
rather than a true reversal of effect over time. Two facts limit the practical
impact: (i) the parametric AFT models reported below do not rely on the PH
assumption and yield the same directional conclusions; the AFT model is
therefore useful as a complementary specification; and (ii) the
\texttt{declared\_duration\_months} hazard ratio (HR = 0.991) is consistent in
sign and magnitude across the univariate, multivariate, and sensitivity
specifications. A fully rigorous treatment would stratify on binned declared

\subsection{Parametric Survival Models}

Three accelerated failure time (AFT) models were fitted to assess parametric
alternatives to the semi-parametric Cox model. Table~\ref{tab:aft} compares
goodness-of-fit (AIC) and concordance across distributional families.

\textbf{Purpose.} AFT models describe the logarithm of survival time directly.
They are useful here because they model time-to-renewal itself rather than only
relative hazard.

\midrule
\textbf{Log-Normal}   & \textbf{7\,068.4} & \textbf{0.655} \\

\textbf{How to read AIC.} AIC is not a $p$-value and not an accuracy score. It
is used only to compare models fitted on the same dataset; lower AIC indicates

models.

The log-normal model achieves the lowest AIC (7\,068.4) and a concordance of
0.655, marginally above the Cox model (0.632). The log-logistic model achieves
the highest concordance (0.660) at the cost of a higher AIC. The Weibull model
performs worst on both metrics. These results suggest that the renewal gap
distribution has a heavier tail than the Weibull's exponential hazard allows,
and that the non-monotone hazard shape of the log-normal/log-logistic models
better captures the data.

\textbf{Limitation.} The AIC comparison only ranks the fitted parametric models
against each other on the same data. It does not establish that the chosen
distribution is the true data-generating process.

\section{Sensitivity and Robustness}

\subsection{Sensitivity Analysis: Model A vs.\ Model B vs.\ Model C}

Three model configurations were compared to assess the impact of low-confidence
links (Section~\ref{sec:reliability}) on the survival estimates. Model~B drops
the LOW tier (composite $< 0.50$); Model~C is the strict margin-based definition
of Section~\ref{sec:margin}, which retains only links that are both high-scoring
and clearly separated from the runner-up.

Because the event variable is constructed by proxy, the main risk is that
low-confidence links distort the survival analysis. The robustness strategy is
therefore to rebuild the event definition under stricter rules and check
whether the main conclusions change. Model~A keeps all accepted proxy events;
Model~B keeps only medium/high composite-score links; Model~C keeps only strict
high-confidence links with high score and clear separation from the runner-up.

\noindent\textbf{Interpretation.} Restricting to high- and medium-confidence

the event rate is 6.2\% and the KM median is not reached, so the apparent
\emph{level} of renewal risk drops sharply, exactly as expected when most proxy

75 events the reduced-covariate Cox model is underpowered and its ranking is
no longer better than chance. The qualitative conclusions --- declared duration
and start year as the dominant predictors, no significant category or procedure
effect --- hold in Models~A and~B, which carry enough events for estimation.
This stress-tests the proxy event definition; it does not validate it as
ground truth. Model~C is a conservative lower bound, not a corrected estimate
of the true event rate.

\subsection{Summary of Phase 2 Findings}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, label=\textbf{F\arabic*.}]

  \item \textbf{Global median renewal time: 50.1 months.} The KM estimate
        is consistent with the dominant 48-month administrative duration
        pattern in public procurement renewal. The distribution is right-skewed:
        roughly 21\% of contracts show an identifiable renewal within 24 months.

  \item \textbf{Two statistically significant predictors.} In the multivariate
        Cox model, only \texttt{declared\_duration\_months} (HR = 0.991,
        $p < 0.001$) and \texttt{start\_year} (HR = 1.071, $p < 0.001$) are
        significant. Shorter declared durations predict faster renewal.
        The positive \texttt{start\_year} hazard ratio should be interpreted
        cautiously: it may reflect calendar-period effects, publication
        practices, and study-window truncation (2021--2024 contracts are
        largely right-censored) rather than a genuine acceleration of
        procurement renewal cycles.

  \item \textbf{At most a marginal category effect; no procedure effect.}
        In the multivariate model, IT Services \& Consulting shows a positive
        effect versus the Cybersecurity reference (HR 1.230, $p = 0.037$);
        all other category and procedure terms are non-significant. The
        category log-rank test is likewise marginal ($p = 0.025$). This is a
        weak signal, not a robust segment effect.

  \item \textbf{Log-normal AFT model preferred.} Among parametric models, the
      log-normal achieves the best trade-off between fit (AIC = 7\,068.4) and
        concordance (0.655), outperforming the Cox model marginally. The Weibull
        model is the worst fit.

  \item \textbf{Sensitivity check supports qualitative stability.} Restricting to
        medium- and high-confidence links (Model B) lifts the KM curve so the
        median is no longer reached, while leaving covariate effects qualitatively
        unchanged. Model C is far more aggressive: censoring all but the 75
        strict high-confidence links collapses the event rate to 6.2\% and the
        KM median is no longer reached. The primary model (Model A) is retained
        as the official result.

\end{enumerate}

The statistical evidence above is mostly internal: threshold sensitivity,
score-margin diagnostics, placebo checks, and covariate balance show stability
and discriminative structure, but they do not estimate the true precision or
recall of the event variable. The manual audit below does, and its verdict is
considerably harsher than the internal checks suggest.

\textbf{Consequence.} The proxy event is \emph{not} validated at the baseline

contracts hide a plausible missed renewal), so the two error types do not
cancel. Recommended re-linking changes: raise the text floor to
$\geq$0.5--0.65, stop scoring generic CPV codes as exact matches, and widen or

\subsection{Operational 12/24-Month Renewal Risk Prediction}
\label{sec:prediction}

\paragraph{Business purpose.} The preceding survival analysis estimates the
\emph{historical} timing of renewals: how long, on average, a digital-sector public
contract takes before an identifiable BOAMP renewal signal appears. This subsection
translates the fitted survival model into \emph{operational} risk indicators for Gigalis.
For each contract still active at a prediction date, it estimates the probability that the
contract generates an identifiable BOAMP renewal signal within the next 12 or 24 months, and
aggregates these probabilities into buyer-level and segment-level rankings to prioritise
renewal-campaign effort.

\paragraph{Prediction model.} The \textbf{log-normal AFT} model of
Section~\ref{sec:phase2} is used as the main prediction model, because it has the best
(lowest) AIC among the parametric survival models (AIC~=~7\,068.4; Table~\ref{tab:aft}). It is
applied with the same covariates and design matrix as the survival analysis. The prediction
date is the study end date, \textbf{2024-12-31}; the contract age at that date is
\(a_i = (\text{2024-12-31} - \texttt{start\_date}_i)\) expressed in months. Predictions are
produced for the 1{,}204 contracts in the model design matrix (the 6 contracts with missing
\texttt{type\_procedure} are excluded, as in the Cox/AFT models).

\paragraph{Conditional renewal probability.} For a contract \(i\) with current age \(a_i\),
covariates \(X_i\), and prediction horizon \(h \in \{12,24\}\) months, the conditional
probability of an identifiable renewal within the horizon, given that the contract has not yet
shown one, is
\[
\hat{p}_{i,h}
=
\Pr(T_i \leq a_i+h \mid T_i>a_i, X_i)
=
\frac{\hat{S}(a_i \mid X_i)-\hat{S}(a_i+h \mid X_i)}
     {\hat{S}(a_i \mid X_i)},
\]
where \(T_i\) is the time to an identifiable BOAMP renewal, \(a_i\) is the contract age at the
prediction date, \(h\) is the prediction horizon (12 or 24 months), and
\(\hat{S}(t \mid X_i)\) is the survival probability predicted by the selected log-normal AFT
model. By construction \(\hat{p}_{i,24}\geq\hat{p}_{i,12}\) (the survival function is monotone
non-increasing); probabilities are clipped to \([0,1]\) and the \(0/0\) case
(\(\hat{S}(a_i)\approx 0\)) is set to zero. Across the 1{,}204 contracts the 12-month
probability ranges from 0.003 to 0.288 (mean 0.075) and the 24-month probability from 0.023
to 0.564 (mean 0.216); the expected number of identifiable renewals is 89.7 within 12 months
and 259.9 within 24 months. (These are markedly lower than at the earlier $W=12$ setting,
where the expected 12-month count was 208: the narrower $W=6$ window shifts the fitted
model toward lower conditional renewal probabilities.)

\textbf{How to read the result.} This probability is conditional. It does not
ask, ``Will the contract renew within 12 months from its start date?'' It asks,
``Given that this contract has reached its current age without an observed
renewal, what is the probability that an identifiable BOAMP renewal appears
within the next 12 or 24 months?''

At the buyer level, the expected number of renewals is the sum of the
contract-level probabilities:
\[
E[\text{renewals}_{12\mathrm{m}} \text{ for buyer } b]
= \sum_{i \in b} \hat{p}_{i,12}.
\]
This is why a buyer with many moderate-risk contracts can rank above a buyer
with one high-risk contract.

\paragraph{Risk tiers.} Contracts are assigned an operational risk tier from
\(\hat{p}_{i,12}\): \textbf{High} (\(\geq 0.50\)), \textbf{Medium} (\(0.25 \le p < 0.50\)),
and \textbf{Low} (\(p < 0.25\)). On the current data this yields 0 High, 6
Medium, and 1{,}198 Low contracts: because most contracts are already several years old, no
contract reaches a 12-month conditional probability of 0.50. These are \emph{operational}
thresholds, to be calibrated later with the supervisor and with the manual-validation sample.

\paragraph{Limitation.} These probabilities estimate the risk of an identifiable BOAMP renewal

not as legally verified renewal probabilities. The manual audit
(Section~\ref{sec:manual_audit}) estimates the precision of the proxy event at
only $\approx$9--15\%, so these indicators inherit that uncertainty and should
be recalibrated after the recommended re-linking.
