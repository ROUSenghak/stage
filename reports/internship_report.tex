\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This report presents the work carried out during a data-science internship for
\textbf{Gigalis}, a public-interest group and central purchasing body in the
Pays de la Loire region. The goal is to move the analysis of public digital
procurement from a descriptive view (``what happened?'') towards a predictive
one (``when will a contract be re-tendered?''). The central methodological
obstacle is that the French procurement publication system (BOAMP) contains
\emph{no explicit field} marking whether a call for tenders renews an earlier
contract. The renewal relationship must therefore be reconstructed from
observable signals. We build an auditable, reproducible \emph{proxy} renewal
event using Sentence-Transformer text embeddings combined with CPV, temporal,
and buyer signals, and we use it as the event variable for survival analysis.
On 1{,}100 eligible source contracts the linking method recovers a plausible
renewal for 697 of them (\textbf{63.4\%}), against an 11.3\% lexical baseline.
Survival modeling on this dataset gives a global Kaplan--Meier median survival
of \textbf{48 months}, a Cox proportional-hazards concordance of
\textbf{0.6528}, and a best-fitting log-normal parametric model. We report these
results honestly together with their limitations: the renewal event is a proxy,
buyer identification is weak, and two originally planned components (a trained
NLP classifier and change-point detection) are not yet implemented.
\end{abstract}

% ===========================================================================
\section{Introduction and Problem Statement}

\subsection{Context}
Gigalis is a \emph{groupement d'int\'er\^et public} that operates a central
purchasing body for digital services in the Pays de la Loire region. As part of
building a ``data control tower'', the organisation wants to anticipate when its
digital contracts (software, IT services, telecommunications, security) are
likely to come up for renewal, rather than only describing past activity. The
practical value is operational: a reliable renewal forecast helps plan
re-tendering workloads and negotiate from a stronger position.

\subsection{The core difficulty}
Public procurement in France is published through two main open-data channels:
\textbf{BOAMP} (\emph{Bulletin officiel des annonces des march\'es publics}, the
tender and award notices) and \textbf{DECP} (\emph{Donn\'ees essentielles de la
commande publique}, the awarded-contract records). Neither source contains a
\emph{renewal link}: a notice does not state ``this tender replaces contract
\textit{X} that is expiring''. Yet the renewal event is exactly the quantity we
want to model. The problem is therefore twofold:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Event reconstruction.} Infer, from text, classification, timing,
        and buyer identity, which later notice is the renewal of an earlier
        contract --- producing a binary \texttt{event} variable and a survival
        time.
  \item \textbf{Survival modeling.} Given this reconstructed event, estimate how
        the probability of renewal evolves over time and which contract
        characteristics drive it.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Research question and scope}
\emph{Given a digital procurement contract, when is it likely to be renewed or
re-tendered, and which factors influence that timing?} The study is deliberately
scoped to keep the data tractable and the modeling honest:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Sectors:} CPV divisions 48 (software), 72 (IT services),
        32 (telecom), 35 (security).
  \item \textbf{Territory:} departments 44, 49, 53, 72, 85 (Pays de la Loire).
  \item \textbf{Period:} 2015--2024.
  \item \textbf{Official modeling path:} \textbf{BOAMP-only}. DECP is explored
        as a richer but non-linkable source and kept for future enrichment
        (Section~\ref{sec:data}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Data and Scope}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Sources}
\paragraph{BOAMP (official path).} BOAMP is queried through the Opendatasoft
Explore API (dataset \texttt{boamp}). After filtering to the target CPV
divisions, territory, and period, the acquisition yields \textbf{3{,}181
notices}. One technical subtlety is that CPV codes are embedded inside a
JSON-encoded field in \emph{two} different serialisations: the legacy BOAMP
format (notices up to 2023) and the EU eForms/UBL format (2024 onward); both are
parsed and cross-checked. From the notices, the \texttt{APPEL\_OFFRE} (call for
tenders) records define the survival population: \textbf{1{,}933 source
contracts}.

\paragraph{DECP (exploratory only).} DECP is obtained from the consolidated
tabular file on data.gouv.fr (\textasciitilde210~MB). It contains
\textbf{3{,}039} digital contracts for the same scope and is far better
populated than BOAMP (100\% SIRET identifiers, 95.6\% amounts, 99.4\%
durations). However, BOAMP and DECP \emph{do not share a reliable common key},
so a robust contract-level merge is not achievable. DECP is therefore documented
and retained as a future enrichment path, but it is \textbf{not} part of the
official BOAMP-only modeling handoff.

\subsection{Taxonomy}
To analyse renewal by technology family, each contract is tagged with one of ten
categories (CAT01--CAT10: Software \& Applications, IT Services \& Consulting,
Telecom \& Networks, Cybersecurity, Cloud \& Infrastructure, Data \& AI, etc.).
This taxonomy is a \textbf{deterministic rule-based mapping} from CPV codes and
description keywords --- it is \emph{not} a trained classifier. This is an
important honesty point: the project plan originally foresaw a learned NLP
classifier, but the implemented component is rule-based.

The data-quality assessment drives every downstream modeling choice
(Table~\ref{tab:dq}). The decisive weaknesses are buyer identification and

\section{Method}

The pipeline has four stages: cleaning, renewal-event reconstruction, survival
dataset construction, and survival modeling.

\subsection{Cleaning}
Raw notices are normalised: amounts are filtered for zero, suspiciously small,
and ceiling-flagged values; durations are parsed and, when missing, imputed to a
default (a binary \texttt{dur\_was\_imputed} flag records every imputation);
buyer names are normalised into a \texttt{buyer\_key}; and the taxonomy tags are
applied. The cleaned corpus is the input to linking.

\subsection{Renewal-event reconstruction (core contribution)}
\label{sec:linking}
A renewal is reconstructed by matching each source \texttt{APPEL\_OFFRE} to a
later candidate notice from the same buyer.

\paragraph{Eligibility.} A source contract is \emph{eligible} only if its
expected renewal window (start date plus declared duration) falls inside the
2015--2024 study period. Contracts whose renewal would necessarily occur after
2024-12-31 are right-censored \emph{before} analysis. This reduces the
1{,}933 source contracts to \textbf{1{,}100 eligible} (833 censored upfront).
Restricting evaluation to eligible contracts is what makes the linking rate
meaningful rather than mechanically deflated.

\paragraph{Composite score.} For an eligible source $i$ and candidate $j$, a
composite similarity score combines four signals:
\begin{equation}
  S(i,j) = 0.40\,s_{\text{text}} + 0.25\,s_{\text{cpv}}
         + 0.20\,s_{\text{temp}} + 0.15\,s_{\text{buyer}}.
  \label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item $s_{\text{text}}$: cosine similarity of multilingual Sentence-Transformer
        embeddings (\nolinkurl{paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2}) of the
        contract descriptions. This is the strongest discriminator and the main
        improvement over the lexical baseline.
  \item $s_{\text{cpv}}$: graded CPV compatibility (1.0 identical, 0.7 same
        division, 0.3 same section, 0.5 if either CPV is missing) --- a
        \emph{scored} signal, not a hard filter, because buyers sometimes
        reclassify.
  \item $s_{\text{temp}}$: temporal proximity to the expected renewal date.
  \item $s_{\text{buyer}}$: buyer-identity agreement.
\end{itemize}
Hard filters (same buyer, candidate strictly later, minimum text similarity
$0.20$) prune the candidate set; the best-scoring candidate is then selected.
Accepted links are graded into confidence tiers (low $<0.50$, medium
$0.50$--$0.70$, high $\geq 0.70$), which supports the sensitivity analysis.

\subsection{Survival dataset construction}
Each eligible contract becomes one survival row:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \texttt{event = 1} if a plausible renewal link was found; the survival
        time is the observed gap (months) to the renewal.
  \item \texttt{event = 0} if no link was found before study end; the survival
        time is the censoring time from start date to 2024-12-31.
\end{itemize}
The result, \nolinkurl{boamp_phase2_survival.csv}, has one row per eligible
contract with the following covariates:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \nolinkurl{declared_duration_months}
  \item \nolinkurl{dur_was_imputed}
  \item \nolinkurl{start_year}
  \item category and procedure type
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Survival models}
Using this dataset we fit: (i) non-parametric \textbf{Kaplan--Meier} estimators,
globally and stratified by category, declared-duration group, and imputation
flag, with \textbf{log-rank} tests for differences; (ii) a \textbf{Cox
proportional-hazards} model (univariate and multivariate); and (iii)
\textbf{parametric} accelerated-failure-time models (Weibull, log-normal,
log-logistic) compared by AIC. A sensitivity analysis re-runs the whole modeling
chain on a stricter, high-confidence subset of links.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Renewal linking}
The method links \textbf{697 of 1{,}100} eligible source contracts, a linking
rate of \textbf{63.4\%}, versus \textbf{11.3\%} for the lexical
(Jaccard/TF-IDF) baseline (Table~\ref{tab:link}). The gain comes from two
changes: semantic embeddings instead of lexical overlap, and treating CPV as a
graded score rather than a hard grouping constraint.

\section{Limitations and Honest Appraisal}

The results are useful but bounded, and the boundaries matter:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{The event is a proxy.} \texttt{event = 1} means ``a plausible
        renewal link was found'', not a legally certified renewal. The pipeline
        is designed to be transparent and auditable, not to prove renewal.
  \item \textbf{Weak buyer identification.} Only \textasciitilde13\% of unique
        buyers carry a SIRET; the rest are matched by normalised name, which can
        split one public entity (e.g.\ ``Ville de Nantes'' vs ``Nantes
        M\'etropole'') into several keys and create false negatives. The SIRET
        field appears \emph{only} in 2024 eForms notices, which are all censored
        upfront, so the modeled cohort is matched by name alone --- SIRET
        enrichment can help future data but not the historical window.
  \item \textbf{Short-text false positives.} Generic descriptions (``prestations
        informatiques'') can match spuriously despite the semantic embeddings.
  \item \textbf{Structural censoring.} Contracts started in 2021--2024 are
        largely censored, since a typical 48-month contract would renew after the
        2024-12-31 cut-off; the positive \texttt{start\_year} hazard partly
        reflects this.
  \item \textbf{Modest discrimination.} A concordance around 0.65 means useful
        but far-from-perfect ranking; predictions should inform planning, not
        replace judgement.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Not implemented.} For full transparency, two components of the
original project plan are \emph{not} part of this study: a \textbf{trained NLP
classifier} (the taxonomy here is rule-based) and \textbf{change-point / trend
detection}. The DECP linking remains at the lexical baseline only; the
Sentence-Transformer method was applied to BOAMP, not yet to DECP.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}

This internship delivered an end-to-end, reproducible pipeline that turns raw
BOAMP notices into a survival-ready dataset and a first predictive view of
contract renewal for Gigalis. The key methodological contribution is the
reconstruction of a renewal event from unlabelled procurement notices using
Sentence-Transformer embeddings, lifting the linking rate from 11.3\% to 63.4\%
on eligible contracts. The survival analysis shows that renewal timing is driven
mainly by declared contract duration (median survival 48 months) rather than by
technology family, with a log-normal model giving the best parametric fit.

Future work follows directly from the limitations: (i) apply the
Sentence-Transformer linking method to DECP and attempt a BOAMP--DECP merge for
amount/SIRET enrichment; (ii) strengthen buyer resolution using SIRET and a
controlled directory of regional public entities; (iii) replace the rule-based
taxonomy with a trained text classifier; and (iv) add change-point and trend
detection to monitor shifts in procurement behaviour over time.
